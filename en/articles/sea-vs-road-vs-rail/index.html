Sea, rail, or road

China to Iran has three ways to move freight: sea, rail, road. This page compares them. Price and schedule for all three are quoted on the day.

  • Sea: to Bandar Abbas then inland — the longest, and through the Strait of Hormuz
  • Rail: no direct line to Iran; the CKU railway is not open
  • Road: from Xinjiang, two corridors, about 22 days to the Tehran terminal
  • All three: price and schedule quoted on the day

Sea

To Bandar Abbas, then inland to Tehran. The longest option. It passes the Strait of Hormuz; in tense periods war-risk cover may apply.

Rail

No direct line into Iran. The China–Kyrgyzstan–Uzbekistan railway is under construction and not open; current block trains mostly route via the Caspian or Turkey, a different network.

Road

From Xinjiang on two corridors — Wuqia (via Kyrgyzstan, +0.4% invoice value) and Khorgos (via Kazakhstan, no 0.4%). About 22 days to the Tehran terminal, not the door. See Wuqia or Khorgos and transit time.

Choosing

Urgent, mid-volume, or opening-sensitive cargo goes by road. Very large, non-urgent volume looks at sea. Price and schedule for all three are quoted on the day.

To get a quote, send four things: item name; quantity or weight; destination city; Wuqia or Khorgos.
