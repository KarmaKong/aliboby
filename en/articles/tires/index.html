CARGO: TYRES

Tyres to Iran: weight, counting and lashing before loading

2026-11-19

Tyres behave differently from most cargo on this corridor. Steel tools fill a trailer by weight and leave air behind; flat-pack furniture fills it by volume and leaves weight unused. A trailer of truck tyres tends to reach both ceilings at almost the same moment — which changes how the load is planned, counted and lashed.

  • Ceiling per truck: 22 tonnes and 80 CBM, whichever comes first
  • On truck tyres the two ceilings arrive almost together
  • Count in pieces everywhere: invoice, packing list, carton marks
  • Full trailers only — hub to customs, CPT / DAP

Two ceilings that arrive together

A truck tyre typically weighs somewhere between 50 and 70 kg and takes up roughly 0.2 CBM standing upright; a passenger-car tyre is far lighter and smaller. Worked at those figures, 22 tonnes lands near 350–440 truck tyres and 80 CBM lands near 400 — the two numbers sit within a few per cent of each other. Two consequences follow. The trailer is neither half-empty nor over the limit when the load stops, and a small error in the declared weight (banding, pallets, wooden dunnage) is enough to push it over. Measure both figures; do not derive one from the other.

Passenger and light-truck tyres flip the picture: volume arrives first, and nesting — one tyre inside another — is what keeps a small shipment from paying for air. If your cargo is mixed, work out the two counts separately and tell us which ceiling you expect to hit.

Counting: pieces, sets, bundles

Tyre shipments are held up by arithmetic more often than by weight. Sellers quote in sets of four, factories band in fives or tens, the invoice says sets, the packing list says bundles, and the declaration needs a piece count that matches the HS code and the value. Pick pieces as the single unit and carry it everywhere: invoice, packing list, carton marks and the enquiry you send us. If the invoice is written in sets, put the conversion on the invoice itself rather than in a separate message.

A trailer of tyres is several hundred identical-looking black objects. The marks on the bundles are the only way to reconcile what the broker counts at destination with what left the factory, so keep the numbering with the packing list rather than in the driver's phone.

Weight and where it sits

Gross weight means tyres plus banding plus pallets plus any dunnage. Banding on tyre bundles is steel or PET and it adds up over hundreds of pieces. Axle position matters more here than on bulky cargo, because a tyre load is compact: stacking everything at the rear to make unloading easier leaves the tractor axles light and the trailer tail heavy. Spread the load along the bed, keep the densest bundles low and ahead of the rear axle group, and send us bundle weights in advance if the whole shipment is one dense block — the loading plan sets the positions, not the driver at the ramp.

Lashing round cargo on a curtain-side trailer

A tyre bundle is round in one direction and flat in the other: it rolls when it stands on its tread and slides when it lies flat. A curtain-side trailer has no steel wall for the load to lean on, so the plan needs chocks under the first row, straps over every stack, and an answer for the transshipment point, where the load is taken out and put back on a different trailer. Bands that held at the factory often do not survive one transshipment; count on re-strapping and say so before loading. Lashing, reinforcement and any crane lift are billed separately from the freight.

Brand, model and what gets reviewed

Tyres are sold under trade names, and much of what leaves China carries one. Cargo with a brand, logo or moulded sidewall marking is reviewed before any agreement; we do not opine on authenticity or selling rights, we ask for the documents the shipper holds. Several brands in one trailer is normal — list each brand with its quantity on the packing list instead of writing "tyres" on one line. Tyres themselves are general cargo: no battery, no safety data sheet, no dangerous-goods declaration. The review is commercial, not chemical.

Odour and co-loading

New tyres give off rubber odour for weeks. In a full-trailer shipment the cargo is usually one shipper's, but if tyres travel with textiles, cartons or food-grade packaging in the same trailer, that odour transfers. Load tyres toward the rear, keep absorbent packaging away from them, and if the co-loaded goods are odour-sensitive, say so at the enquiry stage so the loading order is set in advance.

Route, entry point and current transit time

There are two exits: Wuqia, through Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan; and Khorgos, through Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan. On the Iranian side, Sarakhs and Lotfabad are entry points — not destinations and not places of clearance. The sellable destinations are Mashhad customs or West Tehran customs, and Iranian clearance is handled by the buyer's licensed broker, not by us.

Wuqia transit time is counted from the Wuqia border and excludes Iranian clearance. Currently a direct truck takes 21–23 days to Tehran customs and 18–21 days to Mashhad customs; a transshipment truck takes 26–30 and 24–28 days. The run from the China hub to the Wuqia border is about 7 days and is counted separately. The Khorgos line has no published day figure; it is given in the written quote. These are current ranges, not a commitment.

What to send for a quote

Six things: origin hub, destination customs, goods description with HS code, gross weight in kilograms, volume in CBM, and bundle count with outer dimensions. For tyres add three lines: piece count, the brands on the packing list, and whether the bundles are banded at the factory. With that list a written rate is issued with a validity date. Freight is not on this page.

Frequent questions

Can a tyre load reach both limits at once?

On truck tyres it usually does. Weight and volume run close to each other, so measure gross weight and CBM separately instead of estimating one from the other.

Do you count tyres in sets or in pieces?

In pieces. The invoice, the packing list and the marks on the bundles all carry the same piece count; if the seller quotes in sets, the conversion is printed on the invoice.

Are tyres dangerous goods?

No. Tyres are general cargo with no battery and no safety data sheet. Branded tyres are still reviewed before any agreement, and we ask for the documents the shipper holds.

Do you clear customs in Iran?

No. Delivery is at the destination customs, CPT / DAP, and clearance is done by the buyer's licensed broker. We do not clear Iranian customs and we do not deliver to the buyer's warehouse door.
