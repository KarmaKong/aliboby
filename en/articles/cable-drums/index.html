CARGO: CABLE DRUMS

Cable drums to Iran: diameter, weight and lashing before loading

2026-11-21

A cable drum is not a crate with a cable inside it. It comes on a wooden or plywood reel, it is round in one direction and flat in the other, it stands on a narrow rim, and it cannot be nested. Three numbers — diameter, width, gross weight — decide whether a trailer carries twelve drums or four, and whether the load is legal at all.

  • Ceiling per truck: 22 tonnes and 80 CBM, whichever comes first
  • Diameter, width and drum weight are needed before the quote
  • Lash over the flange, never across the cable core
  • Full trailers only — hub to customs, CPT / DAP

Three numbers before a quote

The reel decides the loading, not the cable. Diameter sets the stacking height and whether the drum rolls onto the bed at all; width sets how many rows fit across; gross weight per drum sets how much of the 22 tonnes one piece consumes. A 2.5 m drum and a 1.2 m drum of the same cable are different cargo. Put all three on the packing list next to the cable type, the cross-section in mm² and the metres on each drum — the loader needs the weight, and the broker at destination reconciles metres against value.

Two further lines save a second round of questions: whether the drums may be laid flat, and whether they may be stacked. Drums wound for upright storage only cost height; drums that can lie flat cost bed length. Either way the answer changes the plan, so it belongs in the first message rather than in a phone call from the ramp.

Weight arrives first

On cable the weight ceiling usually comes long before the volume ceiling. Copper and aluminium are dense and the reel is not light either: a large steel-reinforced drum of power cable can weigh two to three tonnes. At that size eight drums are around 20 tonnes and the trailer is finished while the deck still looks half used. It is the opposite problem to flat-pack furniture. Give us the drum count and the weight of the heaviest drum and the loading plan starts from the point load rather than from cubic metres.

The air between the drums

Eighty CBM is a box; drums are circles. Whatever sits between them carries nothing, so treating each drum as diameter × diameter × width overstates what the trailer really holds — the four corners of that rectangle are empty. Two consequences follow. A trailer of drums is often limited by bed length and axle load before 80 CBM is reached, and when a shipment is discussed per CBM the figure should be the measured block as it will ship, not the sum of the reels. Photographs of the packed drums and the dimensions of the block are worth more than a calculated number.

Lashing a round load on a curtain-side trailer

A drum rolls on its rim and on its flange. A strap that passes across the middle of the reel presses on the cable and does nothing to stop rotation; the strap has to sit over the flange, with wedges or chocks on both sides of the first and last drum in each row. A curtain-side trailer has no steel wall for the load to lean against, so the restraint has to be self-contained rather than the side of the trailer. Drums also creep: a strap that is tight in the yard is slack after one border, which is why the lashing is checked again at each crossing and at the transshipment point.

Transshipment and the second lift

The Wuqia line changes trailers at Bukhara, and a drum lashed at the factory is released and lashed again there. Banding and wedges from the factory are usually consumed by the first lift. Moving a two-tonne reel takes a crane or a forklift with a drum attachment, and that lift is billed separately from the freight. A drum is handled more often than a crate on the same route; say so at the enquiry stage so the cost is visible before the cargo leaves the factory.

Axle position and point loads

A drum concentrates its weight on a narrow strip of deck. Four three-tonne drums in a row over one axle group are not the same load as twelve tonnes spread along the bed, even though the gross weight matches. Heaviest drums low and ahead of the rear axle group, rows spread so no single point takes more than the deck is built for. If the whole shipment is one block of drums, send the weight per drum and the order in which they come off the truck at destination: the unloading sequence sets the loading order, and re-shuffling on the ramp is not free.

Route, entry point and current transit time

There are two exits: Wuqia, through Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan; and Khorgos, through Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan. On the Iranian side, Sarakhs and Lotfabad are entry points — not destinations and not places of clearance. The sellable destinations are Mashhad customs or West Tehran customs, and Iranian clearance is handled by the buyer's licensed broker, not by us.

Wuqia transit time is counted from the Wuqia border and excludes Iranian clearance. Currently a direct truck takes 21–23 days to Tehran customs and 18–21 days to Mashhad customs; a transshipment truck takes 26–30 and 24–28 days. The run from the China hub to the Wuqia border is about 7 days and is counted separately. The Khorgos line has no published day figure; it is given in the written quote. These are current ranges, not a commitment.

What to send for a quote

Six things: origin hub, destination customs, goods description with HS code, gross weight in kilograms, volume in CBM, and package count with outer dimensions. For cable drums add five lines: number of drums, diameter and width of the largest, gross weight per drum, whether the drums may lie flat, and whether they may be stacked. With that list a written rate is issued with a validity date. Freight is not on this page.

Frequent questions

Will a very large drum fit on a trailer?

That is a question about the trailer, not about the cable. Send the diameter, the width and the gross weight of the largest drum and the answer is given against a specific trailer; a drum wider than the bed is a different case from one that is only tall.

Is the crane lift included in the freight?

No. Reinforcement, lashing and any crane or forklift lift are separate lines, quoted so the total is visible before the cargo moves.

Are cable drums dangerous goods?

No. Power and control cable is general cargo: no battery, no safety data sheet. Branded cable is still reviewed before any agreement and we ask for the documents the shipper holds.

Do you clear customs in Iran?

No. Delivery is at the destination customs, CPT / DAP, and clearance is done by the buyer's licensed broker. We do not clear Iranian customs and we do not deliver to the buyer's warehouse door.
